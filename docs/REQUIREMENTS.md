# REQUIREMENTS.md — Ryan's founding words, verbatim

Owner: Ryan, via the Lead. This file is never paraphrased into something else —
if it needs updating, add to it or quote a new statement; don't summarize away
the original words. It exists because the founding requirements for this project
were spoken, never written down, and were lost when a session compacted. That
must not happen again.

## The founding statement (2026-08-31)

> "I need you to help me figure out how to plan, how to build this team in a way
> that actually works. There's a thousand AI video editing apps out there that
> claim to do all of the work, but they don't do almost any of it. This one
> needs to be different. And the way that we be different is by taking it in
> small tiny steps. So we need to figure out the overall structure of what we
> want the app to do or the team to do, and then figure out what those roles
> look like. And then we need to teach each skill one by one, test it, ensure it
> works and works well. And then from there, we build the next role and the next
> skill. And only then, when we build every single role and every single skill
> we could possibly need for this, then do we pull it all together to be one
> cohesive team."

> "I don't need a yes, man. I don't need a cheerleader... I need you to actually
> be an intelligent, self thinking being. I need you to help figure out where
> some of the gaps might be."

> "I want that assistant editor to be able to go into the folder of footage,
> grab all of the pieces of footage that we want, put them on a timeline so I
> have all of the pieces as the final editor. I would have all of the pieces to
> work with and already know what I'm working with. I wouldn't have to go
> searching for footage. I wouldn't have to go searching for sound effects. I
> wouldn't have to go searching for music. I wouldn't have to spend a bunch of
> time culling the footage... I would just have all of the pieces for the
> specific story that we're telling on the timeline."

> "As much of it can be taken off of my plate we're gonna do... up to this
> point, we've had multiple attempts of you taking my idea for how to edit a
> video or create an app or whatever it is, and then you go off for three or
> four hours editing or making something that is virtually completely
> unusable... it doesn't help me at all. So we're trying to do something way
> better than that."

## The five roles, in full (2026-08-31)

> "I think it's best to think of this as kind of like a post production house.
> How post production houses work, typically, it's not one person that does
> everything start to finish. We have somebody who goes out and shoots and
> captures footage — that's on the production side of things. All of that
> footage is then brought and delivered... to the postproduction house. **The
> project manager will ingest all of that footage and organize it properly**
> based on whatever needs to be organized, whether it's the client and the
> project, location, name, date, type of footage, etcetera.
>
> Next, that moves to the assistant editor. **The assistant editor's job is to
> take that footage... and pull all of the usable portions.** So they're
> keeping an eye out for pieces of footage that shake or are out of focus or
> have issues, and they're cutting out all of that... just because it's one
> footage clip doesn't mean we're only pulling that footage clip onto the
> timeline one time. We're pulling all of the usable in and out points of that
> footage that don't include shake and blur and messiness onto that timeline...
>
> And once it's done that, the editor will go through and **organize that
> footage based on the content that's in it** — maybe based on location, maybe
> based on activity... Then they would go through and grab any of the audio.
> They would also make sure the audio... would be **synced to any of the
> A-roll**. That would be a sequence called **all footage synced**...
>
> then that editor would... **transcribe everything**... this editor would
> probably go through and maybe **flag things by pulling up usable pieces of
> information that were interesting or help to inform the story**... they might
> **color code it based on the storyline being told**...
>
> that's essentially the assistant editor's job — to make sure everything is
> organized so that by the time it gets to the lead editor, the creative
> editor, **they're not wasting time going through all of this raw footage**...
>
> the creative editor will then figure out based on all of the pieces that are
> marked from the transcript... and based on the content of the b-roll shots
> that they have... **decide what the story is that they're telling**...
> **build out the game plan for this most ideal version of the story**...
> **pull in music choices based on the tone**, add sound effects, and
> **create the entire first cut of the edit that is ninety five percent done.**
> They would then show that to their supervisor... **repeat this process until
> the supervisor gave them the go ahead**...
>
> once they have **picture lock**... the project would move to the
> **colorist**... bring up anything that was too dark, bring down anything that
> was too bright, cover highlights... and add some kind of look for the overall
> artistic tone...
>
> Once that's finished, the colorist would hand it off to the **audio
> designer**, and the audio designer would analyze all of the levels... and
> then export the final deliverable.
>
> This is how production houses work, and this is what we're trying to
> essentially create... Ideally, I am the supervisor, and I can make
> adjustments, but it would be ideal to work together to get all of these
> roles filled out by AI skills and agents to cover most of the editing side
> of things as possible."

> "Being as this is so complex... I think handling this role by role is gonna
> be a really good call... basically taking the user through each of the
> roles... **It's gonna be probably, um, I'd say, seventy five percent of the
> difficulties we're gonna run into are gonna be in that [assistant editor]
> role.**"

## The overwhelm and the actual goal (2026-09-02)

> "The intention is to take the overwhelming and mundane nonsense of video
> editing, and bypass as much of it as I can so that we can just dig into the
> fine-tuning and the creative spin on stuff... right now as it stands, **I am
> too overwhelmed to edit. I have 4 TB of footage that I have to go through and
> I don't have any real direction on any of it** on what I need to be making —
> it's a bit overwhelming to just have a sea of footage that needs to be gone
> through, organized, create ideas for it, put those ideas together, find the
> B-roll — all of those things. It's too much. So the idea of this app is that
> **I need to take as many of these tasks off of my plate as possible**."

## The four named PreCut shortcomings (2026-09-02) — the most important passage

> "I solved a lot of these issues with the PreCut app. However, I still see a
> lot of work that needs to be done in relation to some of the areas that
> PreCut falls short...
>
> **1. Storyline-finding skims and misses material.** When it comes to picking
> out and finding storylines to build and throw together based on transcripts,
> that's a great idea in theory — where it falls short is the app or AI tends
> to **skim through transcripts and that leaves a lot on the table so that we
> end up missing a lot of the story that matters.**
>
> **2. The fix should be two modes.** I know from a cost saving standpoint it
> makes sense for the AI not to be completely going through every word of every
> transcript all the time, but I think we somehow find a way to bridge the two —
> whether it's by having **different buttons for the amount of work we want**:
> if I have an idea of what I'm wanting to do, I can write a little note of
> 'this is what the video is, these are the pieces I remember from the day,
> this is the overall idea,' and it goes in piece by piece and pulls those
> instead of going through the entire transcript. The alternative is the other
> button — 'I have no idea what to do with this, here are the pieces of
> information, this is the goal' — and it goes through the entirety of the
> transcripts.
>
> **3. Trust.** I haven't been completely sold on any of the ideas that I've
> been fed so far out of the AI suggestions, so I'm a little unsure how much I
> can trust the judgment of the AI that we're building to create these
> stories.
>
> **4. Coverage gap.** There's all of the other roles that I told you about
> that aren't necessarily part of PreCut — which is everything after the
> Creative Editor's job."

And, correcting the record on what PreCut actually already does (2026-09-03):

> "PreCut generates ideas based on the transcript and selects the portions of
> that transcript it wants to use for that idea and then pulls those selects
> onto a timeline fully synced."

> "The HGTV Episode was longform and needed more attention. As you noticed
> precut was primarily built for shorter form content."

## Editorial criteria he stated directly (for whichever role eventually judges footage)

> "The things I'm looking for as I cut: movement (a good shot has intentional
> movement even if it's not moving)... I'm looking for a clear focus point
> (this can include a rack focus)."

> "A clip that pans left to right over a few seconds without repetitive up and
> down motion would be an intentional pan. A clip that goes left to right over
> half a second or one second is likely camera jerk... b-roll shot at 60fps
> should be interpreted to 29.97 or 23.976, which would make it slowmo."

These criteria were tried against real footage in Phase 4 and the resulting
detector did not beat "keep everything" — see ROADMAP.md's Decision Log for
2026-09-02. They're recorded here because they're still true statements of what
he's looking for, independent of whether any current technique can act on them.

unchanging record of why, in Ryan's own words, so that record survives context
loss, agent handoffs, and time.
